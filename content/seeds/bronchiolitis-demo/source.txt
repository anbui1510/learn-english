Bronchiolitis is a common lower respiratory tract infection in infants, most often caused by respiratory syncytial virus.
Typical age at presentation is under 12 months, with peak severity between 1 and 3 months of age.
Caregivers usually report a few days of coryza followed by cough, fast breathing, and feeding difficulty.
Clinical examination may show tachypnea, chest wall recession, widespread crackles, and expiratory wheeze.
Diagnosis is clinical; routine chest radiography and laboratory tests are not required for uncomplicated cases.
Pulse oximetry helps guide oxygen therapy; supplemental oxygen is considered when saturations remain low in air.
Supportive care is the mainstay: hydration, nasal saline and suctioning, and monitoring work of breathing.
Bronchodilators, corticosteroids, and antibiotics do not routinely improve outcomes in typical viral bronchiolitis.
Infants with apnea, persistent hypoxemia, or inadequate feeding may need hospital admission and close observation.
Parents should be advised about expected illness course of several days and when to seek urgent review for worsening distress.
High-risk groups include premature infants and those with congenital heart disease or chronic lung disease.
Hand hygiene and avoiding smoke exposure reduce transmission risk within the household.
